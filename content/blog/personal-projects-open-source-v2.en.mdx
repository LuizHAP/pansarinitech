## Introduction to Open Source Transition

Building personal projects is a common journey for software engineers, often starting as hobbies or side projects. However, transitioning these projects into open-source contributions can have profound impacts. The open-source model fosters innovation, collaboration, and growth beyond the constraints of a single developer's capabilities or imagination. This post explores how my personal projects matured into open-source endeavors, revealing challenges faced, lessons learned, and technical decisions that guided this path.

## The Genesis of DoAção

One of my projects, **DoAção**, originated as a localized community platform to encourage volunteer work. Developed with **Next.js**, **Supabase**, and **Drizzle ORM**, it aims to connect volunteers with businesses in Jundiaí, allowing volunteers to earn and redeem points for their contributions. The shift from a private project to an open-source initiative was driven by community interest and the potential for broader impact.

<Note>Creating a robust real-time point tallying system was a challenge that required careful balance between accuracy and performance.</Note>

Conversion into open source meant revisiting architectural decisions, such as action verification flows and **Supabase RLS policies**, to ensure the system was both scalable and secure for an open audience.

## Pricing Innovation with MTG Price Monitor

**MTG Price Monitor** leverages **Next.js**, **Drizzle ORM**, and **Discord.js** to track Magic: The Gathering card prices, alerting users via Discord. Transitioning this into an open-source project highlighted several issues. Scraping API data reliably without breaching rate limits was non-trivial and demanded an adaptable alert deduplication logic.

### Addressing Rate Limit Challenges

Understanding and navigating various marketplace API rate limits was crucial. The use of **Discord.js** required compliance with Discord's own rate limiting, necessitating precise scheduling and data caching strategies. This was pivotal to prevent service interruptions while maintaining a smooth user experience.

## Navigating Technical Trade-offs

Each decision in transitioning to open source was underpinned by trade-offs. In **NotificaMe**, a smart queue management system, choosing between **Redis** queues and **PostgreSQL** persistence was crucial. While Redis offered speed, PostgreSQL's durability was a priority for data persistence, especially given the queuing and notification scope.

<Warning>An unoptimized choice between Redis and PostgreSQL can lead to either performance bottlenecks or data loss risks, depending on the use case.</Warning>

The microservices architecture supported scalability but introduced complexity in orchestration and deployment.

## Strategic Open Source Engagements

Engaging with the broader community played a critical role in shaping these projects. Open feedback cycles were immensely valuable, leading to enhancements in functionality and performance that I wouldn't have achieved alone. Furthermore, careful consideration of licensing ensured that while usage was open and encouraged, rights and contributions were respected.

<InlineBadge variant="primary">Stable</InlineBadge> releases improved adoptability, while consistent documentation maintained accessibility for new and experienced contributors alike.

## Conclusion

Transitioning personal projects to open source is rarely a straightforward journey but rather a series of strategic decisions compounded by technical challenges. Through DoAção, MTG Price Monitor, and NotificaMe, I've experienced firsthand the rewards of this transformative process. Open-source projects not only enhance personal growth but also contribute to the collective innovation and diversity of the software development community.

For those considering this path, start small, stay open to feedback, and continuously iterate—open source is as much about community as it is about code.
